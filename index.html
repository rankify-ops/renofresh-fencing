How much does a new fence cost in Wellington?
It depends on length, height, material, how sloped the ground is and whether an old fence has to come out — which is why online per-metre prices are rarely what people end up paying. We’ll look at your boundary and give you a free written quote that covers as much as possible upfront. If anything outside the agreed scope comes up, we discuss it and get your approval before we go ahead. No surprises on the invoice.
Will it actually stand up to Wellington wind?
That’s exactly what we design for. On exposed sites we often recommend hit & miss fencing, where boards alternate either side of the rails so wind passes through the gaps and dramatically less load hits the fence. Aluminium won’t warp, twist or rot in heavy rain. And whatever you choose, posts are concreted in and timber is treated for outdoor and ground-contact exposure.
My section is sloped. Can you still do a nice-looking fence?
Yes — Wellington’s hills are our normal. Aluminium raking panels pivot to match the exact gradient for a continuous line instead of a staircase look, and timber fences can follow the natural contour of the land. See our Crofton Downs project for a steep, narrow example.
Timber or aluminium — which should I choose?
Timber paling is strong, cost-effective, fully private and easy to repair or extend later — great value for long boundaries. Aluminium costs more upfront but is rust-proof, very strong and practically maintenance-free, and it’s ideal for slopes, pools and coastal air. PVC is a clean, low-upkeep middle ground. Tell us your priorities and we’ll give you an honest recommendation.
It’s a shared boundary — does my neighbour have to pay half?
Under New Zealand’s Fencing Act 1978, neighbours are generally expected to share the cost of an adequate boundary fence, and there’s a formal fencing-notice process if you can’t agree. Most people start with a friendly chat — and a clear written quote makes that conversation a lot easier. For disputes, check the guidance on govt.nz or get legal advice.
Do I need council consent for a fence?
Most residential fences up to 2.5 m high don’t need a building consent under the Building Act. Pool fencing has its own safety rules, and your district plan can still limit height in some spots, like road boundaries. Mention anything you’re unsure about when we quote.
Can you remove my old fence?
Replacing an ageing fence is one of our most common jobs. Just mention it in the form (“Replace an old fence”) and we’ll include it in your quote so everything’s in one price.
My fence came down in a storm. Can you help?
Yes. We secure the site, work out why it failed — often a retaining wall or drainage issue, not just the fence — and rebuild it stronger. Choose “Repair / storm damage” in the form or call 04 888 9788.
